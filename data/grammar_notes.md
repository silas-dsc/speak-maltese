# Maltese quick reference

## 1. Alphabet & sounds

Maltese has 30 letters. The ones that trip English speakers up:

| Letter | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `ċ` | *ch* in **ch**urch | `ċavetta` (key) |
| `ġ` | *j* in **j**am | `ġobon` (cheese) |
| `g` | hard *g* in **g**o | `gallarija` (balcony) |
| `għ` | silent, but **lengthens the neighbouring vowel** and adds a slight pharyngeal throatiness | `għandi` ≈ *aandi* |
| `h` | silent at the end of a word, lightly aspirated elsewhere | `hu` ≈ *oo* |
| `ħ` | strong breathy *h*, from the throat | `ħobż` ≈ *hobz* |
| `j` | *y* in **y**es | `jien` ≈ *yen* |
| `q` | **glottal stop** — a clean catch in the throat, like the Cockney *bu'er* | `qalb` ≈ *ʔalb* |
| `x` | *sh* in **sh**e | `xemx` ≈ *shemsh* |
| `z` | *ts* in ca**ts** | `zokkor` ≈ *tsokkor* |
| `ż` | *z* in **z**oo | `żmien` ≈ *zmien* |
| `ie` | one long vowel, roughly *ee-eh* | `ilsien`, `żmien` |

Stress usually falls on the **penultimate syllable**: `KA-fè` → *kafè* is an exception
(loanword, final stress), `TAJ-jeb`, `sa-BIĦ` (final because of the long vowel).

## 2. The definite article `il-`

`il-` = *the*. Two things happen to it:

**Assimilation** before the "sun letters" `ċ d n r s t x z ż`:

- `id-dar` (the house), `ir-raġel` (the man), `is-sena` (the year)
- `it-triq` (the road), `ix-xemx` (the sun), `iż-żmien` (the time)
- `iċ-ċavetta` (the key), `in-nar` (the fire)

**Elision** before a vowel or `h`/`għ`: `l-ikel`, `l-omm`, `l-ilma`, `l-għodwa`.

Before a word starting with two consonants an `i-` is inserted: `skola` → `l-iskola`,
`sptar` → `l-isptar`.

Prepositions fuse with it:

| | + `il-` |
|---|---|
| `fi` (in) | `fil-` — `fil-Belt` |
| `bi` (with) | `bil-` — `bil-Malti` |
| `ta'` (of) | `tal-` — `tal-baħar` |
| `minn` (from) | `mill-` — `mill-Awstralja` |
| `ma'` (with) | `mal-` — `mal-ħbieb` |
| `għal` (for) | `għall-` — `għall-festa` |
| `sa` (until) | `sal-` — `sal-pjazza` |

## 3. Negation

Verbs are wrapped: **`ma` + verb + `-x`**.

- `nifhem` → `ma nifhimx` (I don't understand)
- `naf` → `ma nafx` (I don't know)
- `għandi` → `m'għandix` (I don't have)

Non-verbs use `mhux`: `mhux tajjeb` (not good), `mhux jien` (not me).

`qatt`, `xejn`, `ħadd` still take the `ma ... x` frame:
`qatt ma kont hemm` (I've never been there), `ma naf xejn` (I know nothing).

## 4. Verbs

There is **no infinitive**. The dictionary form is the 3rd-person masculine singular
perfect (`kiteb` = *he wrote*). The present tense is built with prefixes:

| Person | Prefix | `kiteb` (write) | `fehem` (understand) |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | `n-` | `nikteb` | `nifhem` |
| you (sg) | `t-` | `tikteb` | `tifhem` |
| he | `j-` | `jikteb` | `jifhem` |
| she | `t-` | `tikteb` | `tifhem` |
| we | `n- -u` | `niktbu` | `nifhmu` |
| you (pl) | `t- -u` | `tiktbu` | `tifhmu` |
| they | `j- -u` | `jiktbu` | `jifhmu` |

Two very common particles sit in front of the present tense:

- **`qed`** (or `qiegħed`) = progressive → `qed naqra` (I *am* reading)
- **`ħa`** / `se` / `sa` = near future → `ħa mmur` (I'm going to go)

The perfect (past) uses suffixes instead: `ktibt` (I wrote), `ktibna` (we wrote),
`kiteb` (he wrote), `kitbu` (they wrote).

Imperatives are common and short: `ejja` (come), `mur` (go), `ħu` (take), `agħti`
(give), `ara` (look), `isma'` (listen), `oqgħod` (stay/sit), `dur` (turn).

## 5. "To be" and "to have"

There is **no present-tense verb "to be"** — you just juxtapose:

- `Jien Malti.` — I am Maltese.
- `Il-ktieb fuq il-mejda.` — The book is on the table.
- `Kif int?` — How are you?

Past: `kont` (I was), `kien` (he was), `kienet` (she was), `konna` (we were).

"To have" is the preposition `għand` + pronoun suffix:

`għandi` (I have) · `għandek` (you have) · `għandu` (he has) · `għandha` (she has) ·
`għandna` (we have) · `għandkom` (you pl have) · `għandhom` (they have)

It also carries states: `għandi l-ġuħ` (I'm hungry), `għandi tletin sena` (I'm 30).

## 6. Possessives

Two strategies:

1. **Suffix on the noun** (mostly kinship & body parts): `ommi` (my mother),
   `ibni` (my son), `idejja` (my hands), `sieqi` (my foot).
2. **`ta'` + pronoun**, the general-purpose one: `il-ktieb tiegħi` (my book),
   `id-dar tagħha` (her house).

`tiegħi, tiegħek, tiegħu, tagħha, tagħna, tagħkom, tagħhom`

## 7. Nouns: gender & plurals

Two genders. Most nouns ending in `-a` are feminine (`mejda`, `karozza`); most others
masculine. Adjectives agree: `raġel twil` / `mara twila` / `nies twal`.

Plurals come in two flavours:

- **Sound plurals** — add `-i`, `-jiet`, `-in`: `siġġu → siġġijiet`, `Malti → Maltin`
- **Broken plurals** — the word re-shapes internally (Semitic): `ktieb → kotba`,
  `dar → djar`, `triq → toroq`, `raġel → irġiel`, `mara → nisa`, `tifel → tfal`

There is also a **dual** for pairs and measures: `idejn` (two hands), `għajnejn` (two
eyes), `jumejn` (two days), `sagħtejn` (two hours).

## 8. Numbers with nouns

Numbers 2–10 take special short forms before a noun:

- `żewġt itfal` (two children), `tliet kotba` (three books), `erba' snin` (four years)
- `ħames ewro`, `sitt xhur`, `seba' ijiem`, `tmien kmamar`, `disa' persuni`, `għaxar minuti`
- `mitt` before a noun (`mitt ewro`), `mija` standing alone

Above 10 the noun goes singular: `ħdax-il sena` (eleven years), `għoxrin ewro`.

## 9. Word order

Default **VSO/SVO** flexibility, but for a learner **subject–verb–object** is always
safe: `Jien nixrob il-kafè.` Adjectives follow the noun: `dar kbira`, `ħobż frisk`.

Questions are made with a question word up front, or by intonation alone:
`Trid kafè?` (Do you want coffee?)

## 10. Register note

Everyday Maltese mixes in a lot of English and Italian, and code-switching is normal
and native — `orrajt`, `ċaw`, `grazzi`, `mela`, `prosit`, `skużi`. Don't treat that as
an error. But for practice, prefer the Maltese word when one exists.

## 11. Ten things learners get wrong

1. Forgetting the `-x` on negatives: ✗ `ma nifhem` → ✓ `ma nifhimx`
2. Using a "to be" verb in the present: ✗ `Jien inkun għajjien` → ✓ `Jien għajjien`
3. Not assimilating the article: ✗ `il-dar` → ✓ `id-dar`
4. Not fusing preposition + article: ✗ `fi il-Belt` → ✓ `fil-Belt`
5. Putting the adjective first: ✗ `kbira dar` → ✓ `dar kbira`
6. Using `għandi` for age/hunger wrongly: ✓ `għandi tletin sena`, ✓ `għandi l-ġuħ`
7. Sound plural where a broken plural is needed: ✗ `ktiebi` → ✓ `kotba`
8. Ignoring the special counting forms: ✗ `tnejn tfal` → ✓ `żewġt itfal`
9. Pronouncing `q` as English *k* instead of a glottal stop
10. Pronouncing `għ` as a consonant instead of lengthening the vowel
